# Food and cooking
I learned to cook from watching my mother, and I'm still learning now. She never used a recipe — she'd taste, add a little of this, a little of that, and somehow it always came out right. I need the recipe in front of me, but I'm getting braver about changing things as I go.

Sunday is when I do the serious cooking. I'll spend an hour or two in the kitchen making something that takes its time — a soup, a curry, or a tray of roasted vegetables. The house fills up with the smell of it, and by the time I sit down, I've already eaten half a meal just from tasting.

During the week it's simpler. Eggs in the morning, something quick at lunch, and dinner is whatever the fridge suggests. I've started planning meals on Saturday, which saves money and stops me from ordering takeout every other night. It's amazing how much easier the week feels when you know what's for dinner.

My kitchen is small, but I know where everything is. The good knife lives on the magnetic strip, the spices sit in the cupboard above the stove, and the heavy pot waits at the back for the weekend. When friends come over, they always end up in the kitchen with me, and that's how I like it.

There's one dish I make when everything goes wrong — pasta with garlic, olive oil, and whatever cheese is in the fridge. It takes ten minutes and it has never let me down. I've eaten it after long days, bad news, and cancelled plans, and it always feels like a small act of kindness.

Cooking has taught me patience more than anything else. You can't rush a stew, and bread rises at its own pace. Somewhere between the chopping and the waiting, my head clears. By the time I sit down to eat, the day's problems usually look smaller than they did an hour before.
