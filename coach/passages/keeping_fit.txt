# Keeping fit
I'm not the kind of person who loves exercise, but I've learned to love how it makes me feel. Three mornings a week, I put on my running shoes and go out before I can talk myself out of it. The first five minutes are always the worst — after that, something shifts, and my legs find their rhythm.

My route runs through the park and along the canal. There are ducks that expect bread from every jogger, and an old man who does tai chi by the bridge, rain or shine. We nod at each other. We've never spoken, but we know each other's schedule better than most friends do.

At the weekend I take a longer walk, no hurry in it at all. Sometimes a friend joins me and we talk the whole way; sometimes I go alone and let my thoughts wander. Walking is where I do my best thinking. Problems that seemed huge at my desk turn out to be much smaller on a hillside.

I used to go to the gym, and I hated nearly all of it. The machines, the mirrors, the music — none of it worked for me. What finally made exercise stick was giving up on the idea of a perfect routine. Now I just move when I can: a run, a swim, a long walk, a bicycle ride to the market instead of the bus.

Some weeks are better than others. When work gets busy, exercise is the first thing to slip, and I feel it — in my back, my sleep, my mood. That's how I know it isn't optional anymore. It's not about looking different; it's about feeling like myself.

If I've learned one thing, it's this: start smaller than you think you need to. Ten minutes counts. A walk to the corner counts. The habit matters more than the distance, and the habit only grows if you make it easy to say yes to.
